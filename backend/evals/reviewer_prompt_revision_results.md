# Reviewer prompt revision: vague and polished Google snapshots

## Scope and provenance

Changed only `EVIDENCE_PROMPT` in `project_question_planner.py`. The experimental relevance/question
generator, coordinator, validators, model, and production V2 reviewer were held fixed. Three prompt
revisions were measured on both previously approved snapshots: six paid experimental runs. Each
artifact embeds its exact planner source, raw requests/responses, and frozen production baseline.

These are **Codex assessments, not independent human labels or a blind evaluation**. Existing
reference actions were preserved for before/after counts. The polished and vague original runs
have separately analyzed Google contexts; within each before/after comparison the context is frozen.
This is a diagnostic development set, not held-out evidence of generalization. No answers or edits
were generated by these comparisons.

## What changed

- Treat ownership and substance as separate judgments. Strong verbs claim ownership without
  proving that the implementation description is informative. A tool list does not establish
  personal implementation after "worked on".
- Recognize a missing change behind a claimed improvement, and distinguish a technique's intended
  benefit from its actual application.
- Stop once concrete functionality, an applied method, a behavior/boundary, or verification makes
  the work identifiable. Do not require all of these, internals for every component, or metrics.
- Require a specific missing factual clause. Keep multiple independent gaps available; do not
  split a broad recovery question and a narrow recovery question into two supposed gaps.
- Give REWRITE an actual wording problem instead of "add specifics" when facts are absent.
- Use illustrative exports, caching, booking, access-control, and rate-limiting examples. Production
  identity, citation, ownership, and evidence enforcement were not changed.

Revision 1 made the reviewer demand internals on already concrete work. Its vague coordinator failed

Revision 2 recovered vague validation and background recovery, but incorrectly treated PostgreSQL's
"worked on" plus tools as owned implementation. Revision 3 recovered that distinction but lost the
validation question again. These are different prompts with one observation each, not repeats from
which to estimate stability.

## Final questions, rated by Codex

### Vague run

1. **Useful:** "What specific parts of the PostgreSQL backend did you implement or change?"
2. **Useful:** "What specific changes did you make to improve the background processing for longer
   tailoring tasks?"
3. **Duplicate of the more specific point-cloud question:** "What specific parts of the
   autonomous-driving stack did you personally implement or change?"
4. **Useful:** "What specific parts of the point-cloud processing did you personally implement
   or change?"

All four were selected. Selected usefulness: 3/4 by this review. This improves precision but still
misses opportunities on vague JobMatcha bullets; the baseline had more useful questions in total.

### Polished run

1. **Duplicate:** "What specific parts of the autonomous-driving stack did you personally
   implement or change?"
2. **Useful:** "Which of the tasks related to Velodyne VLP-16 point-cloud filtering and other
   processes did you personally implement or change?"
3. **Low value for this resume/job:** "What specific quoting and accounting workflows did you
   automate, and what changes did you implement?"

All three were selected. The first two likely elicit the same personal implementation facts; global
coordination still does not reliably remove that overlap. The third asks for more process depth in
a bullet already naming the work and method.

## Remaining work

The reviewer is measurably better at the PostgreSQL/worker distinction, but the issue is only
partially improved. Do not claim it is solved or promote the experimental reviewer from this run.
The missed idempotency mechanism, inconsistent validation-boundary detection, unnecessary Asian
Granite work, and retained ROS overlap remain open. The next measurement should inspect these
specific decisions with fixed expectations and additional resumes/JDs, rather than assuming more
questions or fewer questions alone means better review.
